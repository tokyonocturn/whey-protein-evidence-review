# Heavy metals, microbes, pesticides and other contaminants

Two independent testing programmes dominate the public evidence on protein powder contamination:
the Clean Label Project's 2025 category study and Consumer Reports' 2025-26 investigations. They
disagree on magnitude and agree on direction: **plant proteins are worse than whey, chocolate is
worse than vanilla, and organic is not safer.**

## What the independent labs found

### Clean Label Project, Protein Study 2.0 (January 2025)

- **160 products** from 70 top-selling US brands, about 83% of the market.
- **35,862 individual analyses** for lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, BPA, BPS and pesticides, run at
  Ellipse Analytics.
- **47%** of products exceeded at least one federal or state safety level (including California
  Prop 65).
- **21%** exceeded **twice** the Prop 65 lead level.
- By category, share exceeding the Prop 65 lead benchmark:
  - plant-based **77%**
  - certified organic **79%**
  - chocolate-flavoured **65%**
  - **whey 28%**
  - collagen 26%

The report's own recommendation is telling: the products with the least lead are **whey or collagen
based and not chocolate flavoured**.

### Consumer Reports (October 2025)

- **23 products** (powders and ready-to-drink shakes).
- **More than two-thirds** contained more lead in one serving than CR's level of concern of
  **0.5 µg/day**; about **70%** exceeded 120% of that level.
- Plant-based products averaged **nine times** the lead of dairy-based products and twice that of
  beef-based ones.
- Highest: Naked Nutrition Vegan Mass Gainer at **7.7 µg/serving**, Huel Black Edition at **6.3 µg**.
- Two products exceeded 2.2 µg, the FDA's interim reference level for total daily lead in children.
- Two products exceeded CR's cadmium limit (4.1 µg/day); one exceeded its arsenic limit (7 µg/day).

### Consumer Reports follow-up (January 2026)

Five reader-requested chocolate powders, all lower than the first round:

| Product | Lead per serving | Share of CR's 0.5 µg/day level |
|---|---|---|
| Clean Simple Eats whey | 0.21 µg | 42% |
| Equate whey | 0.27 µg | 55% |
| Premier Protein | 0.38 µg | 77% |
| Truvani plant | 0.46 µg | 93% |
| Ritual plant | 0.53 µg | 107% |

This is the most useful single result in the literature: **low-lead products are manufacturable at
mass-market prices.** Equate is a Walmart brand.

### Clean Label Project "Clean Sixteen"

Sixteen products were **non-detect for all four heavy metals**. Twelve are whey/dairy:

- Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey, Vanilla Ice Cream
- Dymatize ISO100 Hydrolyzed, Gourmet Vanilla
- Isopure Zero Carb, Unflavoured
- Premier Protein 100% Whey, Vanilla Milkshake
- Body Fortress Super Advanced, Vanilla
- GNC Pro Performance 100% Whey, Banana Cream
- Cellucor Whey Protein, Vanilla
- Garden of Life Certified Grass Fed Whey, Vanilla
- Bulk Supplements Whey Protein Isolate
- Wellbeing Nutrition Whey Protein Isolate, Unflavoured
- Nutrabox 100% Whey, Mango
- Wicked Protein Clear Whey Isolate, Cherry Limeade

Vanilla and unflavoured dominate. That is not a coincidence; it is the cocoa effect.

## Why whey is cleaner than plant protein

Plants take up cadmium and lead from soil and accumulate them in seeds. Cocoa is a particularly
strong accumulator. Whey is a filtered animal protein: the contaminants that remain are diluted by
the filtration and spray-drying process, and dairy cows do not concentrate heavy metals in milk the
way plants concentrate them in seeds.

The organic paradox follows: organic plant proteins are grown without synthetic pesticides but still
take up metals from soil, and organic certification does not test for heavy metals. That is why
**79% of organic products exceeded the Prop 65 lead benchmark**, higher than conventional.

## How worried should you be?

The benchmarks used are deliberately conservative:

- **Prop 65 MADL for lead: 0.5 µg/day.** California sets this to protect against reproductive harm
  with a large safety margin. It is not a toxicity threshold.
- **FDA interim reference level for lead: 2.2 µg/day for children**, higher for adults.
- **CR's cadmium limit: 4.1 µg/day; arsenic 7 µg/day.**
- There is **no enforceable FDA lead limit for protein powders or dietary supplements**. CR
  petitioned the FDA to set one in October 2025.

Context matters. Lead has no safe level, and cumulative exposure matters, so lower is better. But
the exposure from a protein powder at 0.2-0.5 µg/serving is small compared with major dietary
sources and is not a reason to panic. The rational response is **relative**: choose products that
test low, choose vanilla over chocolate, and do not assume a premium price or an organic label means
cleaner.

A simple exposure calculation: one serving per day at 0.5 µg = 182.5 µg/year. One serving per day at
0.2 µg = 73 µg/year. Over a decade that is a meaningful difference in cumulative lead, which is why
product choice matters more than brand loyalty.

## Microbial contamination

Low-moisture dairy powders are not sterile, and pathogens survive in them for long periods.

- **Bacillus cereus** is the characteristic spore-former in whey powder. A 2023 review reported that
  62.5% of US dairy powders in cited surveys tested positive; a 2025 Brazilian survey of 35 whey
  protein products found presumptive *B. cereus* in **60%** of samples (mean 2.47 log CFU/g) and
  spores in **63%** (mean 2.30 log CFU/g).
- Counts of 1-3 log CFU/g are low and usually harmless for healthy adults, but *B. cereus* can cause
  vomiting or diarrhoea, particularly when a powder is mixed and left at room temperature.
- **Salmonella** recalls in dry whey and whey isolate are rare but real: AMPI recalled dry whey in
  2018 (precautionary; all shipped product tested negative) and Vitalabs recalled whey isolates in
  2011.
- In February 2026 the FDA warned **Hearthy Foods** that its ready-to-eat whey protein isolate was
  adulterated because it was prepared under insanitary conditions and the facility had not analysed
  *Salmonella* and *Listeria* recontamination as hazards.
- Plant protein surveys (CFIA, 245 samples) found *Salmonella* in none, *B. cereus* at elevated
  levels in 4.1%, and 95.1% satisfactory overall.

Practical advice: keep powder dry, don't mix a shake and leave it warm for hours, and discard tubs
that smell off or clump unexpectedly.

## Pesticides

Public data are thin. The Clean Label Project ran pesticide panels as part of its 35,862 analyses
and did not headline pesticide exceedances; its findings were dominated by heavy metals and
bisphenols. Organic certification restricts synthetic pesticide use on the dairy farm, but the
protein is filtered and spray-dried, and residue levels in milk are already low. Pesticide residues
are a bigger issue for plant proteins, where the seed is the consumed fraction.

There is no good public dataset showing pesticide residues above regulatory limits in whey protein.
That is a statement about missing data, not proof of absence.

## Bisphenols

BPA and BPS are packaging migrants. The Clean Label Project reported a large improvement: bisphenols
were detected in only **3 of 160** products in 2023-24, versus 55% in earlier testing. Tub packaging
matters less than it used to.

## Bottom line

Heavy metals are the real contaminant issue in protein powders, and the ranking is consistent:
**whey beats plant, vanilla beats chocolate, and certification beats price as a signal only when the
certification includes contaminant testing.** Microbial contamination is low-level and manageable
with storage hygiene. Pesticide data are too sparse to rank products. If you use whey daily, choose a
vanilla or unflavoured product that has published low or non-detect heavy metal results, and treat
chocolate as an occasional flavour.
