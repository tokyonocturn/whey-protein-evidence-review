# Recommendations: who should buy what

Recommendations are organised by the decision a person actually faces. Every product named here
appears in `data/products.csv` with a computed cost per 25 g of protein.

## First question: do you need a supplement at all?

Calculate your protein intake for two or three typical days. If you are already at **1.6 g/kg/day**
and training, whey will not add muscle [S01]. If you are below target, especially at breakfast, in
older age, or during a calorie deficit, a shake is a cheap, convenient fix. That is the only
question that matters before brand choice.

## By situation

### The general gym-goer who wants value

**Buy:** Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey, vanilla or chocolate, 5 lb.
**Why:** 24 g protein, WPI-led blend, 5.5 g BCAAs, $1.51 per 25 g, widely certified, and the vanilla
SKU tested non-detect for four heavy metals. It is the benchmark for a reason.
**Alternative:** Myprotein Impact Whey at $1.19 per 25 g if price is the priority and you accept
variable certification by market.

### The lactose-sensitive lifter

**Buy:** Dymatize ISO100 (vanilla) or Transparent Labs Grass-Fed Isolate.
**Why:** WPI at 0.5-1% lactose, 25-28 g protein, low fat, both certified. ISO100 is $2.17 per 25 g;
Transparent Labs is $1.93 and NSF Certified for Sport.
**Alternative:** Isopure Low Carb, or any WPI-dominant blend. Avoid WPC 80 if 1-2 g of lactose
causes symptoms.

### The drug-tested athlete

**Buy:** Informed Sport products (Ascent Native Fuel) or NSF Certified for Sport (Transparent Labs,
Momentous, Thorne, Klean, BPN, Garden of Life Sport).
**Why:** Every-batch or lot-level banned-substance screening is the only meaningful protection
against inadvertent doping.
**Do not:** rely on "banned substance tested" marketing language without a register entry.

### The older adult at risk of inadequate protein

**Buy:** A whey isolate or concentrate with 25-30 g per serving, vanilla or unflavoured, plus a
leucine-rich food at breakfast.
**Why:** 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day is recommended, with 25-30 g per meal to overcome anabolic resistance
[S16]. Whey is easy to chew, digest and afford. Naked Whey ($1.84) or ON Gold Standard ($1.51) work
well; a clinician-channel isolate is not necessary.

### The person with a diagnosed cow's milk protein allergy

**Buy:** nothing in this review.
**Why:** Whey isolate and hydrolysate still contain beta-lactoglobulin and alpha-lactalbumin. Use a
clinician-approved alternative (soy, pea/rice blend, egg white) and read allergen panels.

### The person with kidney disease

**Do not self-prescribe.** KDIGO recommends 0.8 g/kg/day in CKD G3-G5 and advises against intakes
above 1.3 g/kg/day in those at risk of progression [S13]. Any whey use should be counted in the total
and discussed with a renal dietitian.

### The person with cirrhosis

**Increase protein, not decrease it.** 1.2-1.5 g/kg ideal body weight/day is recommended, and protein
should not be restricted in hepatic encephalopathy [S14, S15]. Whey is a reasonable supplement if
appetite is poor.

### The acne-prone young man

**Try:** eliminating whey for 8-12 weeks and observing. The association is real in case-control data
(47% vs 27.7%) but a 6-month RCT did not confirm worsening [S21, S22]. If whey is not the trigger,
reintroduce it; if it is, a plant blend or a lower-dose regimen may help.

### The person who blames whey for bloating

Work through the ladder in doc 06: halve the dose, switch to WPI, switch to unflavoured/no gums, then
try a hydrolysate, then a non-dairy protein. Most people find a tolerated option.

### The budget buyer

**Buy:** Myprotein Impact Whey ($1.19) or Premier Protein ($1.69).
**Avoid:** Body Fortress despite its $0.77 headline, unless you accept that the labelled 30 g may not
all be whey protein.

### The "clean label" buyer

**Buy:** Naked Whey (one ingredient) or Transparent Labs (stevia, NSF Sport).
**Why:** no artificial sweeteners, no gums in Naked, short ingredient lists, certification.
**Know:** you are paying 22-28% more for that preference, and organic does not mean lower heavy
metals.

## Products to treat with caution

| Product | Issue | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Body Fortress Super Advanced Whey | Free amino acids, taurine, creatine; alleged spiking; 69.8% protein density | Buy only if price dominates, and discount the protein number |
| BSN Syntha-6 | 47.8% protein density, 227 kcal per 25 g protein | Treat as a shake/meal replacement, not a protein concentrate |
| Chocolate SKUs used daily | Cocoa drives cadmium/lead; 65% of chocolate powders exceeded Prop 65 lead in CLP testing | Use vanilla/unflavoured daily; chocolate occasionally |
| Any "whey" listing glycine/taurine/BCAA blend | Nitrogen spiking | Choose a product without free amino acids |
| Uncertified premium isolates above $2.50/25 g | You are paying for brand | Compare against a certified $1.93 isolate |
| Any whey product if CMPA diagnosed | Contains milk protein | Avoid entirely |

## A simple decision tree

1. **Do you have kidney disease, cirrhosis, CMPA, or are you pregnant?** → clinician first.
2. **Are you drug-tested?** → Informed Sport or NSF Certified for Sport only.
3. **Are you lactose-sensitive?** → WPI.
4. **Do you want the lowest cost per gram?** → concentrate or blend, largest tub.
5. **Do you want the shortest ingredient list?** → Naked Whey or unflavoured isolate.
6. **Do you use it daily?** → vanilla or unflavoured.
7. **Everything else** → Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard, 5 lb.

## Dosing summary

- **Daily target:** 1.6 g/kg (range 1.2-2.2).
- **Per meal:** 0.25-0.4 g/kg, or 25-30 g.
- **Scoop:** most products are 22-25 g; one scoop covers one meal's protein gap for a 60-90 kg adult.
- **Frequency:** 3-5 protein-containing meals, not 5 shakes.
- **Timing:** total intake matters more than the anabolic window.

## What would change these recommendations

- A product-level, independently replicated heavy-metal dataset showing a named product consistently
  non-detect across lots.
- A long-term RCT (>2 years) of very high protein intake and kidney outcomes.
- Evidence that hydrolysate produces better hypertrophy in a well-controlled trial.
- A regulatory change setting an enforceable lead limit for protein powders.
- A major reformulation of any named product.

## Bottom line

Buy a certified, vanilla or unflavoured, 70-85%-protein product at the best price per 25 g of protein
you can verify. For most people that is Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard; for lactose-sensitive
people it is an isolate; for drug-tested athletes it is Informed Sport or NSF Sport; for people with
milk allergy it is nothing in this category. Spend the savings on food.
